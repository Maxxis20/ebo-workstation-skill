AHU01 air handling unit: functional description

Supply and extract air handling unit with rotary heat recovery, heating coil, cooling coil and two pressure controlled fans, run by its own controller on the building's IP network.

1. Scope

This document describes how air handling unit AHU01 is built, how it is connected to the building management system (BMS) and how it is to operate. It is written for the people who design, program, commission and maintain the unit. All values are starting values to be confirmed at commissioning.

Terms: "running" means the unit is in normal operation, night cooling or night heating. Tags (TE2, TF01 ...) are the device tags on the plant and the point names in the controller.

3. Control system

3.1 Structure

The building has a distributed BMS: every plant has its own controller in its plant room, and the controllers share one IP network with the BMS server. AHU01 is run by one SpaceLogic MP-C-36A.

WorkStation / WebStation (operators)
        |
BMS server: graphics, alarm list, trend storage, users, schedules editing
        |
========+============ building automation network, BACnet/IP ===================
        |            |               |               |              |
   MP-C-36A      controllers     controller      controller     room controllers,
   AHU01         of the other    heating plant   cooling plant  IP-IO modules
   (this unit)   air handlers                                   (room sensors)
  • The controller does all control of the unit. Sensors, valves, drives and dampers are wired to its terminals; the control program runs in it with a cycle time of 1 s.
  • The server holds no control logic for the unit. It shows the graphic, collects alarms and trend values, and passes operator actions (setpoints, mode selector, alarm reset) to the controller.
  • Other plant is a peer on the network. Only values that belong to other plant or to the building as a whole cross the network (3.4).

3.3 IP network

  • Every controller has a fixed IP address and a BACnet device instance number that is unique in the building.
  • The controller has two Ethernet ports with a built-in switch. Controllers can be wired one by one to a plant room switch, in a chain from controller to controller, or as a ring so that one broken cable does not cut off the others. The topology is chosen per plant room.
  • The automation network is a segment of its own (separate VLAN or separate switches), not shared with office traffic. Controllers in another IP subnet need BACnet broadcast management (BBMD).
  • Field wiring is conventional: 0-10 V signals, temperature sensors, contacts and relays to the controller terminals.

3.5 Loss of communication

The unit keeps running when the server or the network is down. To be confirmed on site at commissioning by disconnecting the network:

  • Unaffected: start and stop on the schedules, fan pressure control, supply temperature control, coil return water control, pump, freeze protection, heat recovery frost protection, emergency stop, fire contact and every other function that rests on wired inputs.
  • Held at the last value received: outdoor temperature, room temperatures, warm water signal. The functions that use them work on a stale value until the connection returns.
  • Not available: the heat supply alarms cannot arrive, so a fault of the heating plant does not stop the unit; freeze protection on the wired sensors TE1 and TE2 remains. No graphic, no alarm forwarding, no setpoint changes, no reset from the graphic.

Requirements that follow: a communication alarm on the server for the controller and for each source of a network value; an alarm reset button wired to the panel if the unit must be restartable without the server (it then takes a spare input or an IP-IO module).

5. Description of operation

5.1 Operating modes

Mode selectorBehaviour
OffUnit stopped, night modes included. Freeze protection and coil return water control stay active
Manual onUnit runs continuously
AutomaticUnit runs on the running schedule, on the extended run button HS10, and in the night modes
  • Extended run: in automatic, HS10 runs the unit while the contact is closed and for 60 minutes after it opens.
  • Run mode shown to the operator: stopped, normal, night cooling, night heating, stopped by critical alarm.
  • A critical alarm (5.12) stops the unit in every position of the selector.

5.2 Start and stop

  1. On a start command both dampers open at once and temperature control is released.
  2. The extract fan starts after 30 s, the supply fan after 60 s, each at its minimum speed; pressure control then takes over.
  3. In cold weather the start-up pre-heat (5.7) runs before the supply fan starts.
  4. On a stop command dampers close, fans stop and the heat exchanger and the cooling valve go to 0 % at once. There is no fan run-on. The heating valve stays under the coil return water control (5.8) and the pump under its own logic (5.9).

5.3 Season

The unit works in a heating season or a cooling season. In automatic the season follows the mean outdoor temperature of the previous day: cooling season at 11 °C or more, back to heating season at 9 °C or less. It can change once a day, at midnight. The operator can fix either season with the season selector.

The season decides which side of the temperature sequence is available (5.6) and which night mode is allowed (5.11).

5.4 Fans and duct pressure

Each fan holds its duct static pressure at a setpoint: supply 80 Pa (PE1), extract 60 Pa (PE2). The speed rises when the pressure is below the setpoint. Speed limits are 20 ... 60 % for both fans; the controller is limited to the same range so that it cannot wind up behind the limit. The same setpoints apply in normal running and in the night modes.

5.5 Supply temperature setpoint

The supply air setpoint is compensated by the extract air temperature TE7: 21.5 °C at 18 °C extract air or less, falling linearly to 16 °C at 23 °C extract air, and constant above. If TE7 is faulty the setpoint is 19 °C.

In night heating the setpoint is fixed at 27 °C (adjustable 18 ... 35 °C), in night cooling at 17 °C (adjustable 15 ... 25 °C).

5.6 Supply temperature control

The supply temperature TE2 is controlled in sequence. Each stage has its own controller, and one stage is in control at a time; stages before it are held at 100 %, stages after it at 0 %.

OrderHeating sideCooling side (cooling season only)
1Heat exchanger SC3Heat exchanger SC3, only while the extract air is cooler than the outdoor air
2Cooling coil valve TV2 with warm water, only in the heating season and when the cooling plant reports warm waterCooling coil valve TV2
3Heating coil valve TV1
  • Stage change: the next stage is released when the stage in control has been at 100 % for 30 s, and handed back when it has been at 0 % for 30 s. A stage that is not available is left out.
  • Between heating and cooling: the cooling side is entered when all heating outputs are at 0 % and the supply air is more than 0.5 K above the setpoint for 30 s, and left the other way round. In the heating season the cooling side is never entered; in the cooling season the heating side still works.
  • Cooling recovery: when the extract air is at least 2 K cooler than the outdoor air the heat exchanger is used as the first cooling stage and not as a heater; this ends when the extract air is 2 K warmer than the outdoor air.
  • Tuning (gain / integral time): heat exchanger 1.5 / 45 s, cooling coil as heater 2 / 45 s, heating coil 1.5 / 45 s, cooling valve 2.5 / 45 s. Dead zone 0.1 K.

5.7 Start-up pre-heat

When the unit starts in the heating season with an outdoor temperature of 9 °C or less, all heating stages are released at once: heat exchanger to 100 %, cooling coil to 100 % if it carries warm water, heating valve to 75 % (50 % with warm water in the cooling coil). The coil is then warm when the supply fan starts 60 s later, and the controllers continue from these positions. The heat exchanger is held at 100 % for the following 20 minutes to cover the temperature drop at start.

5.8 Heating coil return water and freeze protection

Return water limit. Whether the unit runs or not, a separate controller opens the heating valve as needed to keep the coil return water TE1 at 10 °C or more. It is active while TE1 is below 20 °C and switched off above 22 °C. The heating valve takes the larger of this signal and the sequence signal.

Freeze protection:

  1. Tripped by return water TE1 at 6 °C or less at any time, by supply air TE2 at 6 °C or less while the unit is running, or by a fault of either sensor.
  2. The unit stops: dampers close, fans stop, heat exchanger and cooling valve to 0 %.
  3. The heating valve opens to 100 % and the pump runs.
  4. The state holds until the cause has gone and the alarm has been reset.

5.9 Heating coil pump P1

  • Runs continuously when the outdoor temperature is 9 °C or less, until it rises to 11 °C.
  • The outdoor sensor is not trusted alone. The pump also keeps running while the intake air TE6 is 9 °C or less (until 11 °C; counted only while the unit runs) and while the coil return water TE1 is 12 °C or less (until 14 °C). A fault of TE6 or TE1 keeps it running.
  • Otherwise runs whenever the heating valve is open, and during freeze protection. Run-on 5 minutes.
  • Exercised once a week for 60 s (Monday 10:00).
  • No running feedback for 30 s while commanded is a critical alarm: the unit stops and the heating valve opens.

5.10 Heat exchanger: frost protection and efficiency

Frost protection. Ice on the extract side of the rotor shows as a rising pressure drop PDE4. With the intake air TE6 at 0 °C or less, the rotor speed is limited to 50 % when PDE4 is above a limit that follows the extract fan speed (100 Pa at 0 %, 300 Pa at 100 %). The limit is kept for 5 minutes after the condition clears, and it overrides the start-up hold of 5.7.

Efficiency. The supply side temperature efficiency (TE3 - TE6) / (TE7 - TE6) is shown while the rotor runs above 50 % (until it falls below 45 %) and the extract air is at least 3 K warmer than the intake air.

5.11 Night cooling and night heating

Both run only with the selector in automatic, outside normal running and inside the night schedule. The unit then runs as in normal operation, with the fixed supply setpoint of 5.5. Each condition must hold for 60 s.

5.13 Sensor faults and manual operation

  • A temperature reading below -120 °C or above 120 °C is treated as a sensor fault (open or short circuit) and gives an alarm after 3 minutes.
  • Faulty outdoor sensor: 0 °C is used in its place, which keeps pre-heat and the pump on the safe side; the night modes are blocked.
  • Faulty room sensor: left out of the room mean.
  • Faulty TE1 or TE2: freeze protection trips (5.8).
  • Single outputs are operated by hand by forcing the output point from the BMS; the graphic shows a forced point.
